I stopped using AI like a chatbot and started treating it like an operating system.

ChatGPT is my control room. From my phone, I can send a request into a verified workflow instead of opening five different apps and starting over.

Hermes is the persistent operations manager. It keeps the objective in context, runs scheduled checks, and uses GPT-5.6 Sol to decide what deserves attention.

Codex is the engineering layer. If something needs code, testing, or repair, it can do the technical work and verify the result.

I also added workflow auditing, so the system can find broken handoffs, stale jobs, duplicate steps, unnecessary costs, and tasks that should be automated.

For business, it can identify high-intent leads, rank them, and prepare the next best action while I keep final control.

For markets, I built a paper-only trading desk. Live data comes in, hard risk rules are applied, and Hermes tracks decisions before any real money is considered.

The important part is controlled autonomy. Research and reversible work can happen automatically. Money, messages, publishing, and destructive actions stay approval-gated.

For AI people, that means orchestration, persistence, observability, and guardrails. For everyone else, it means less repetitive work, fewer missed opportunities, and more useful work actually getting finished.
